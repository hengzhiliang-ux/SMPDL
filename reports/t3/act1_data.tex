\subsection{Four Degradation Types}\label{sec:act1-types}

Typology of structural response to axiom removal across the corpus
(dosimetry v1.0; Type-I candidates are \emph{scarce and honestly
registered} --- see survivorship caveat below):

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lp{0.58\textwidth}}
\toprule
\textbf{Type} & \textbf{Characteristics and corpus evidence} \\
\midrule
\textbf{I. Brittleness} & Single-point collapse: a bridge axiom exists,
critical dose $k_c=1$, cliff-shaped survival curve. Candidates are
rare (registered honestly): Pasch's axiom for ordered geometry; naive
comprehension for naive set theory. \\
\textbf{II. Redundant robustness} & Equivalent-base reorganization:
the axiom set has redundant equivalent bases; one side derivable from
the other. Group axioms one-sided (left identity + left inverse
$\Leftrightarrow$ right versions); Boolean algebra multi-bases /
Sheffer stroke / Robbins problem; cut-elimination (cut rule
deletable) as the canonical P-layer redundancy. \\
\textbf{III. Layered peeling} & Core--shell structure: outer theorem
groups die, kernel survives; nested substructure chains. Euclid
$-$ parallel $=$ absolute geometry (neutral theorems all survive);
classical $-$ LEM $=$ negative fragment survives (Glivenko); ZFC
tower; field $-$ commutativity $=$ division ring (dimension theory
survives); separation ladder $T_0\ldots T_4$. \\
\textbf{IV. Threshold phase change} & Continuous degradation to a
critical point where collective properties flip. Classical ground:
graph connectivity thresholds (Erd\H{o}s--R\'enyi), $k$-core
emergence, matroid criticality. Axiom-side candidates: decidability
jumps on fragment ladders. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Four degradation types (dosimetry \S3). Type-I scarcity note:
mature axiomatizations almost never show pure brittleness ---
hypothesis (organizing, untested): historical axiom systems are
reverse-engineered from \emph{overlapping} theorem groups; overlap
$\Rightarrow$ built-in redundancy $\Rightarrow$ brittleness rare. A
survivorship-bias caveat is registered (purely brittle structures may
have collapsed before entering the historical record).}
\end{table}

\subsection{Empirical Law 1: Quantifier Syntax Locates Fragility}\label{sec:act1-law1}

Fragility concentrates in $\forall$-constraint axioms (inequalities,
induction schemas, hereditary closure); $\exists$-constructor axioms
(existence of identity, inverses, \ldots) show \textbf{zero fragility
in 15 removals (0/15)}.

\midrule

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Constraint-vs-constructor fragility counts (corpus,

everywhere it is cited.}

\subsection{Empirical Law 2: Invisible Load-Bearing}\label{sec:act1-law2}

Axiomatic load-bearing correlates \emph{weakly} with fame:
commutativity's exclusive load (preregistered three-textbook table-of-contents
structure, ToC \emph{proxy} metric; theorem-level re-audit listed as
future work) is roughly \textbf{three times} that of the inverse law
(exclusive-caliber mean ratio; small samples $n=4/3$; caliber
sensitivity registered in the adjudication file). Phrasing locked to
